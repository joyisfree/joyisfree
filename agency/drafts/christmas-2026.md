### The dispatch table

Two groups, as the plan sets: **WA, NT and remote**, and **everywhere else**. One date for the whole country would be
either too late for remote buyers or too early for everyone else.

For each group and service, Leif sets **his own last dispatch day before Australia Post's earliest published lodgement
date** for that group. His buffer absorbs a sick day, a late pickup or a stock miss without breaking a date he gave a
customer. "Earliest" means the earliest date Australia Post publishes for any destination in the group, because a
group's date has to hold for its hardest destination.

## 3. The emails

Rules for every one of them, each with its reason:

- **No discount, anywhere, at any point.** It protects full price, and a list that learns to wait for a sale stops
  paying full price.
- **`express` consent only, with the sender footer and the one-click unsubscribe** from `opt-in.md`. The rules under
  "What every send already obeys" on the drop-versus-email page apply to every send.
- **"Best chance", never a guarantee.** Every date reads "order by [DATE] for the best chance of delivery before
  Christmas". Never "guaranteed", "arrives by Christmas" or "in time for Christmas" as a promise. Once the parcel is
  lodged, delivery is out of Joyisfree's hands, and a promise it cannot keep is a consumer-law and trust risk.
- **Scarcity only where it is literally true.** The gift says how many there are, because that number was counted.
- **No prize draw.** A draw is a trade promotion lottery, with permit rules that differ by state and conflict between
  sources (`agency/RUN-drop-vs-email.md`). A gift with every order, while stock lasts, is not a draw.
- **Social posts use the same wording.** Any post about these dates on the label's own channels is an original post
  that says "best chance". Nothing is scheduled into X by any tool.

## 4. The three kill rules

Kept as the team wrote them, with how each is read fixed now, before the result, so the comparison cannot be chosen
after the fact. It is the same principle as the drop-versus-email rule: written down and dated before the send.

1. **If the consented list is under 500** (at 0.12%, SUMMARY, that is about 0.6 orders per send), **send only the gift
   guide and the cut-off email.** Read on 15 Nov from the count in section 1. The cut-off email is email 3. Below that
   size each extra send is expected to bring less than one order, and every send costs the list some attention and
   some unsubscribes.
2. **If the 20 Nov guide brings 0 orders by 26 Nov 2026, drop the Black Friday gift-with-purchase email.** Read on the
   morning of Thu 26 Nov from the store's email report: orders attributed to email 1. If it fires, the scheduled
   27 Nov send is cancelled that day. Zero orders from the guide says the list is not buying from email this season,
   and a second sales email in the same week is then more likely to cost unsubscribes than earn orders.
3. **If orders that received the gift do not beat the November median order value by more than the gift's cost by
   31 Dec 2026, there is no gift-with-purchase next year.** Read on 31 Dec. The terms, fixed now:
   - "Orders that received the gift": the median order value of the orders the gift went out with.
   - "The November median order value": the median of orders placed 1 to 26 Nov 2026, before the gift existed.
   - "The gift's cost": the unit cost counted in section 1.
   - The gift passes only if (gift orders' median) − (November median) is greater than the gift's unit cost.
