# The desk's second client: the agency, creators paid only for verified results

From the growth reviewer's cross-check of 28 Oct 2026, kept as a brief; its own plan when the desk runs.

- **The model:** 12 micro-creators over 60 days, each with a unique code; paid only on tracked orders or app sign-ups
  (say 15% of order value, or $5 a sign-up); never for likes, reposts or follows (X bars paid engagement). Cap $600.
- **The checker:** an AI results-checker maps codes to orders and sign-ups, screens fake followers, and writes a weekly
  cost-per-verified-result report. Same discipline as the Prize Gate: a result is counted only when it is real.
- **The rule at day 60:** keep creators costing under 30% of average order value per sale; move their buyers onto
  Joyisfree's own list and app accounts (own the relationship, not the platform).
- **Then the agency:** sell the home-and-garden clients the same pay-on-verified-result terms once the numbers hold.
- **Why it is the win-win edge:** the quester side teaches the funnel from the user's seat; the brand side is where the
  same judgement earns, with no platform between Joyisfree and its people.

## The pilot, concretely (30 Oct 2026)

Run it as one experiment with a stop rule, exactly like the questing.

- **Budget and clock:** $600 hard cap, 60 days, 12 micro-creators (1k to 25k followers) in home, garden and natural
  fibre. No retainers: the whole budget is outcome-priced.
- **The offer:** a unique code per creator. 15% of tracked order value, or $5 per app sign-up that completes onboarding.
  Nothing for reach, likes, reposts or follows — both because X's authenticity policy treats paid engagement as a
  violation and because reach has never correlated with Joyisfree's orders.
- **The instrument:** one row per creator per week — code, posts, clicks, orders, order value, sign-ups, paid,
  cost per verified result. The checker refuses a result it cannot tie to a code and a real order, the same way the
  Prize Gate refuses a prize it cannot verify. Unverifiable is unpaid, and the creator is told that up front.
- **The kill rule:** at day 30, any creator over 30% cost-per-order-value is stopped; at day 60 the programme
  continues only if blended cost per verified order beats paid social (the $30 to $100 band), otherwise it stops and
  the capability is kept.
- **The asset:** every buyer moves onto Joyisfree's own list and app account. The relationship is owned, not rented.
- **Then sell it:** the home-and-garden clients get the same pay-on-verified-result terms, priced on the measured
  numbers rather than a promise.

## Joyisfree as the issuer, not the farmer (one page)

The research keeps pointing the same way: the quester's side of a quest platform pays about $0 to a few tens of
dollars; **the brand's side is where the leverage is**, because the brand sets the rules and keeps the audience.

- **The cheap door:** a Galxe Space is permissionless — an EVM wallet, project details and a bound official email.
  The Basic tier is $0 a month with a 300 USDT/USDC deposit. The paid tiers (Essentials about $999/mo, Growth about
  $1,699/mo, Verified Space 350 USDC/yr) buy sybil prevention and support, and are **not** justified at this stage.
  So the experiment costs a refundable-style deposit and time, not a subscription.
- **What a Joyisfree quest would actually be for:** not tokens. Proof of purchase and membership — an OAT or
  collectible attached to a real garment or a real drop, redeemable for early access to the next release, a
  colourway vote, or a studio credit in the app. The reward is the brand's own product, which costs Joyisfree
  margin rather than cash and cannot be farmed for resale value.
- **The honest caution:** every Galxe case study found is crypto-native, no non-crypto consumer-brand case exists,
  open quests retain under 5% at day 30, and the best-documented fashion precedent is Nike winding down RTFKT and
  selling it. So this is a **small, cheap test of a channel**, not a strategy — and the audience it reaches is there
  for tokens, not linen, until proven otherwise.
- **The decisive question to answer first, before any deposit:** does a single phygital drop to Joyisfree's existing
  list outperform a plain email to the same list? If a quest platform does not beat an email, it is a cost, not a
  channel. That test needs no Space and no deposit.
- **Order of work:** the creator pilot first (real revenue, no new platform), the issuer test only if the brand side
  still looks worth it after it.
